Yes, creating beautiful interfaces is part of UX/UI, but it's 
also about problem-solving, empathy, business approaches, 
and data analysis. These are things that Dribbble can't 
capture and are far more significant than nice graphics.
Another major red flag is who reports to the UX/UI 
department or designer. While this may bother certain 
designers, it can also be defined as a personal preference. 
Some designers are hesitant to report to marketing directors 
or head developers, while others are enthusiastic.
