Far-Away Elements Appear Unrelated, Are
Easily Overlooked
When consumers miss a link, button, or piece of information
th1t is right in front of them, proximity is frequently to
blame. Far-off objects might be easily ignored by task-
focused users who anticipate all relevant information and
interactive elements to be positioned close together
because things separated by whitespace are perceived as
being less linked. Users selectively attend to certain areas of
the screen as they complete their task, and miss things "in
plain sight" because they are outside this focal area. This
behaviour is sometimes referred to as "tunnel vision"
For example, In our mobile usability research, users ore
frequently frustrated when apps demand them to create an
account before they can access content. However, in many
of these designs, account creation can be skipped - this
option is simply hidden in the upper corner of the page, far
away from the major calls to action.
