Interaction cost is a key considerotion in form design, and
you want to keep it as low as possible. You may reduce the
interaction cost by eliminating ambiguity and unnecessarily
complex questions, dividing the form across screens, or
adding a progress indicator - better usability usually
equates to lower interaction costs.
When considering the interaction cost in your form design,
keep in mind that it will vary depending on the user. Each
form element contributes to the interaction cost in some
way, but the true impact of each piece will be determined
by the user.
In general, walking the user around the form while holding
their hand is less difficult for them. Seeing questions in tiny
groups rother than a big list of entry fields is both less
demanding ond less daunting.
On the other hand, wasting time on a simple question
merely because the user isn't sure what the onswer should
be is incredibly inconvenient.
