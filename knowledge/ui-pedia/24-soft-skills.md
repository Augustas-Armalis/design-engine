# Soft Skills

All pages are text only (no figures, verified on p.579 and p.583). This chapter is about the designer, not the interface: it has almost nothing a script can check. Its value to the engine is as a set of behavioural principles for how the brain should work with a brief and with feedback. Rules below are the book's advice restated as imperatives; everything is opinion/advice, not measured fact.

## 1. Core ideas
1. Designing a digital product is hard because no two jobs are the same, so unique problems arise each time; learning from courses/ebooks/YouTube is not enough to be good (p.576).
2. Hard talents alone do not make a designer stand out; soft skills are "essential for landing a job" (p.576).
3. Only stat in the section: per Icons8, **92 percent of decision-makers** say soft skills are as important as, or more important than, technical skills when recruiting UX/UI designers (p.576). Single cited source, not verified.
4. Hard skills are easy to list on a resume; soft skills are hard to quantify and relate to emotional intelligence, personality qualities and situational awareness (p.577).
5. Hard skills named: user research, information architecture, wireframing and prototyping, visual communication (p.577-578).
6. Seven soft skills named: communication, empathy, creativity, problem-solving, open-mindedness/flexibility, passion, love of constructive criticism (p.578-583).
7. Closing claim: soft talents are "just as vital as their technical skills, if not more so"; the best designer combines hard, soft and questioning abilities (p.583).

## 2. Rules

### Hard skills (definitions the book gives, p.577-578)
- **RULE** Do user research: find out what the target audience wants before designing.
  **Why** Design decisions need a stated audience need behind them. **Applies to** all. **Check** visual/process: brief states audience and needs. **Source** (p.577)
- **RULE** Do information architecture: structure data logically, including navigation, labelling and search tools.
  **Why** Content must be findable before it is styled. **Applies to** SaaS app UI | landing page. **Check** every nav/label/search has a defined place in the IA; visual. **Source** (p.577)
- **RULE** Wireframe (a blueprint of each screen) then prototype (testing the product's functionality) before final visuals.
  **Why** Structure and function are validated cheaply before polish. **Applies to** all. **Check** process. **Source** (p.577-578)
- **RULE** Command visual communication: images, typography, layout, icons, colour, design theory.
  **Why** It is the craft layer of UI. **Applies to** all. **Check** visual. **Source** (p.578)

### Soft skills
- **RULE** Be able to state the reasoning behind every design decision, clearly and "boldly", to team members and clients.
  **Why** "No one knows your thoughts better than you"; a good idea with no explanation fails in the presentation. **Applies to** all. **Check** each output includes a short rationale (engine: attach "why" to each design choice). **Source** (p.578-579)
- **RULE** Build empathy: understand the client's and user's wishes, needs, emotions, goals and thoughts, and fold those insights into the design so the result is relevant and useful.
  **Why** A product cannot meet goals the designer does not grasp. **Applies to** all. **Check** process: brief lists user needs. **Source** (p.579-580)
- **RULE** Learn user needs through user interviews, usability testing, surveys, and observation.
  **Why** These are the book's "greatest ways" to get at needs. **Applies to** all. **Check** process. **Source** (p.580)
- **RULE** Pursue unique ideas ("go outside the box") and see everyday objects in new ways; refine creativity by keeping up with current trends and being inspired by other designers' work.
  **Why** Technical skill can be taught; originality is the scarce, sought-after skill, and it improves with exposure. **Applies to** all. **Check** visual: compare against recent output for sameness. **Source** (p.580-581)
- **RULE** Treat design as problem-solving: understand the exact brief, try options, pick the optimal solution after trial and error, address difficulties "from all perspectives", and stay calm when problems appear unexpectedly.
  **Why** Design exists to make things run smoothly and easily for end users. **Applies to** all. **Check** process. **Source** (p.581)
- **RULE** Stay open-minded and adaptable: take on new ideas and others' opinions, adopt new design trends quickly, and expect clients to add requirements or change everything.
  **Why** Narrow-mindedness blocks finding answers or excellent designs. **Applies to** all. **Check** process. **Source** (p.581-582)
- **RULE** Be passionate: a lack of interest "immediately reflects in the design of a product".
  **Why** Careers include difficult workloads, strange hours, and heavy criticism; enthusiasm and desire to learn sustain quality. **Applies to** all. **Check** n/a (opinion). **Source** (p.582)
- **RULE** Accept constructive criticism and adapt designs in response to input; a flawless design does not exist.
  **Why** There are no defined algorithms or mathematics for design and people's goals and requirements keep changing. **Applies to** all. **Check** process: iteration log shows changes made from feedback. **Source** (p.582-583)

## 4. Dos and Don'ts
No visual good/bad pairs in this chapter. Prose contrasts only:
- Do explain the reasoning behind a design; do not just present a design (p.579).
- Do ask users (interviews, surveys, observation); do not assume what they want (p.580).
- Do accept critique and iterate; do not defend a design as "flawless" (p.583).

## 5. Exceptions & judgement calls
- "Technical talents can be taught; creativity emerges from within" (p.580) is stated and then softened in the same passage: creativity "can be refined and sharpened" by trends and others' work. The book contradicts a pure innate-talent view (opinion).
- Soft skills are called as important "if not more so" than hard skills (p.583): authorial opinion backed only by the one Icons8 stat.
- The "hard skills" list (p.577) has numbering residue ("4. Proficiency in visual communication") on p.578 that implies items 1-3 are the bullets above; there are exactly four.
- [engine note] For an automated design brain, the actionable translation is: always capture the audience and goal first (empathy/research), attach a one-line rationale to each major decision (communication), generate alternatives before settling (problem-solving, creativity), and treat founder accept/reject as constructive criticism to be logged and learned from (open-mindedness, criticism). This is not stated in the book.
