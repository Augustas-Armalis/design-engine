# Larpo SaaS visual systems

All rules here are **[team research]** (no founder feedback is recorded in this pack; founder reactions that touch it are in `larpo-hero-system.md`). Source paths relative to `/Users/augustas/dev/larpo/larpo-knowledge-base/inbox/saas-visual-systems-2026-08-28/`. The pack was written on 2026-08-28, before the founder's hero feedback ledger (2026-08-30); where they disagree the ledger and the 2026-08-30 playbook win (see `overlap-and-conflicts.md`).

## 1. Core ideas

1. The best SaaS visuals expose a **mechanism**: what enters, what changes, what the user controls, what leaves in a better state.
2. Four layers must agree before a hero feels authored: product model and proof; typographic voice; logo/identity family; one brand-specific visual behaviour.
3. Do not ask one prompt to invent company, strategy, copy, logo, product UI, layout and shader at once; follow the ten-step pipeline.
4. **Familiar frame + one product-specific visual behaviour + disciplined identity system** beats six ambient effects.
5. A fictional company still needs internal truth (facts, sample data, a limitation, a reason it can exist now), labelled as sample.
6. An effect that could be recoloured and sold to five unrelated SaaS companies is a library demo, not a signature visual.

## 2. Rules

### 2a. Construction method

- **RULE [LSV-01]** [team research] Write before opening a design tool: "For [user in situation], [product] turns [specific input] into [specific outcome] by [mechanism], while the user controls [important decision]." The sentence implies the product visual (e.g. changed assumptions flowing from source records into a review queue), not an abstract orb.
  **Why** Forces the visual to expose a mechanism.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills, landing page. **Check** sentence present in `DIRECTION.md`. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Begin with the operating model".

- **RULE [LSV-02]** [team research] Follow the pipeline in order: invent/verify product facts; write visitor, problem, mechanism, outcome, objection, proof, action; define three traits + one productive contradiction; choose and prove a typographic voice with real words and glyphs; build one believable workflow and its states; develop a logo from a semantic territory; choose one signature behaviour; compose around one focal order; render at the actual posting ratio and responsive widths; run anti-AI, accessibility, performance and originality gates.
  **Why** One-shot prompts average everything.
  **Applies to** process. **Check** n/a (process). **Source** `README.md` "One governing pipeline".

- **RULE [LSV-03]** [team research] Use five layers: strategic (visitor, painful moment, desired change, first objection, proof, action commitment); brand (three traits + one contradiction, translated into observable behaviour: "calm" may mean stable layouts, low motion amplitude, generous blank space, plain action language, not automatically beige); product (one workflow mapped as states: source/input, processing, draft, human review, outcome, recovery/error/history); identity (wordmark and symbol behaviour, typefaces, colour roles, spacing density, corner and line logic, image/illustration grammar, motion grammar, one signature motif); marketing composition (chosen only after the others).
  **Why** Composition follows proof.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `DIRECTION.md` has all five sections. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "The five-layer system".

- **RULE [LSV-04]** [team research] Choose a hero structure only after the four earlier layers exist; options: split proposition + live product stage; product-first crop with copy anchored in its quiet region; sequential input-to-output strip; editorial statement + one concrete proof object; asymmetric system diagram that doubles as the demo; full-bleed authored image with restrained interface annotation. Do not choose centred copy above a browser frame because it is easiest to generate.
  **Why** Structure must fit the proof.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Marketing-composition layer".

- **RULE [LSV-05]** [team research] Keep product density and marketing density separate: preserve believable UI scale, crop around one task, remove chrome from the composition not from the fictional product model, use callouts only for state or causality, let marketing type carry the broad claim and product type the operational detail. Do not enlarge every app control for marketing.
  **Why** Product needs operational density; page needs negative space.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** product-UI text sizes plausible (e.g. 12-14 px body inside a 1920 frame), `visual`. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Product and marketing use different densities".

- **RULE [LSV-06]** [team research] Keep the frame familiar and choose one deviation that belongs only to this product (formula: familiar page frame + product-specific visual behaviour + disciplined identity system). Examples: source rows that braid into one approved record; hero type that reveals prior states along the baseline; field lines bending around protected data; a page grid that compresses like a capacity model.
  **Why** One strong deviation is more memorable than six ambient effects.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** one sentence in `DIRECTION.md` (LAI-32). **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "The authored-deviation rule".

- **RULE [LSV-07]** [team research] Rank before styling: (1) category or transformation, (2) product proof, (3) primary action, (4) supporting explanation, (5) brand detail. The visual may make proof first and headline second, but the order must be intentional and survive squint, grayscale and the first static frame.
  **Why** Focal order.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** squint/grayscale test. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Hero focal order".

- **RULE [LSV-08]** [team research] For 1440 x 1024 social concepts (earlier frame, superseded for X stills by LHS-30): design the real viewport not a clipped taller page; keep essential content inside an approximate 1280 x 900 safe composition; make the upper 760-880 px complete enough to stand alone if the platform crops; use real anti-aliased fonts and export at 2x when possible then downsample; check at 25% scale; do not add browser chrome, a grid or grain merely to look "designed".
  **Why** Cropping and feed scale destroy fragile compositions.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills (1440 x 1024 frame only). **Check** viewport 1440x1024; content bbox within 1280x900. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Screenshot concept workflow".

- **RULE [LSV-09]** [team research] Use the strongest evidence available: (1) real interactive product behaviour, (2) real product screenshot with truthful data, (3) deterministic prototype with a labelled sample workflow, (4) annotated before/after with explicit assumptions, (5) diagram of the actual mechanism, (6) abstract visual metaphor. Abstract visuals are weakest as proof but can be strongest as identity; keep those jobs separate.
  **Why** Evidence hierarchy.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills, landing page. **Check** evidence tier recorded. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Evidence hierarchy".

- **RULE [LSV-10]** [team research] A fictional company needs recorded internal truth: product name and pronunciation; category and narrow wedge; primary user and current workaround; input, transformation, output and human-control points; pricing logic or sales motion; plausible sample dataset; one limitation or tradeoff; one reason it can exist now. Anything invented is labelled sample. Do not invent customer logos, testimonials, compliance marks, security certifications, user counts or revenue metrics.
  **Why** Internal truth makes the screenshot read as a real company without lying.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** facts file exists; no logo wall or certification badge in the DOM. **Source** `README.md` "Standard for fictional concepts".

- **RULE [LSV-11]** [team research] Failure modes to avoid: a product name chosen only because it sounds like a startup; a logo drawn before a semantic territory; a font chosen from a screenshot not tested with the real name; fake charts that do not support the promise; three font families + five radii + several effect languages competing for "premium"; an elaborate shader that makes the CTA unreadable; a screenshot impressive only at full size that collapses to gray noise in a feed.
  **Why** Documented failure list.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `01-saas-visual-construction.md` "Failure modes".

### 2b. Typography

- **RULE [LSV-20]** [team research] A typeface must pass two tests: semantic fit (structure supports the brand voice) and operational fit (weights, glyphs, metrics, numerals, features, files, licence). Never choose from a single large word; test the company name, headline, paragraph, navigation, form labels, prices, dates, code, long names, punctuation and the Lithuanian set `Ą Č Ę Ė Į Š Ų Ū Ž ą č ę ė į š ų ū ž`.
  **Why** One word hides missing glyphs and weak small sizes.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** render a specimen with the Lithuanian set; `visual`. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` opening.

- **RULE [LSV-21]** [team research] Translate traits to type properties: precise -> regular rhythm, clear apertures, stable numerals, modest contrast (risk: sterile neo-grotesk); human -> open counters, varied shapes, true italics, softer terminals (risk: childish); editorial -> contrast, proportions, italics, optical sizes (risk: fashion-magazine imitation); technical -> distinct glyphs, mono companion, tabular figures (risk: code aesthetic everywhere); established -> controlled proportions, broad family (risk: generic corporate); experimental -> unusual width, axis behaviour (risk: legibility). A serif is not automatically trustworthy and a rounded sans is not automatically friendly.
  **Why** Categories are not personality guarantees.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Convert brand traits".

- **RULE [LSV-22]** [team research] Assign type by role: display, heading, copy, UI label, data (tabular numerals when alignment changes), code/technical (mono only where it conveys structure). One family can do all roles; a second is justified by useful contrast or functional specialisation; a third is exceptional.
  **Why** Roles beat arbitrary sizes.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** <= 2 families (3 only with reason); role table. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Role model".

- **RULE [LSV-23]** [team research] Record for every candidate font: source and exact licence; language/glyph coverage; static vs variable; weight/width/optical-size/slant/grade axes; true italics; small-text clarity and large-size character; x-height; numerals (proportional, tabular, oldstyle, slashed zero); ambiguous pairs `I l 1`, `O 0`, `rn m`, `cl d`; punctuation, arrows, currency, fractions, symbols; wordmark potential and modification rights; WOFF2 availability and payload; fallback metric similarity; whether it is over-associated with AI/SaaS templates.
  **Why** Operational fit sheet.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** sheet in project. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Font evaluation sheet".

- **RULE [LSV-24]** [team research] Do not reject a common font solely because it is common; reject an unconsidered composition. Inter or Geist can work when layout, writing, spacing, product visual and identity carry specificity.
  **Why** Source-internal nuance (opposes the stricter LHS-36 for fictional subjects).
  **Applies to** all. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Font evaluation sheet" note.

- **RULE [LSV-25]** [team research] Pair by role and controlled contrast: decide which family carries character and which carries sustained reading/dense UI; compare x-height, cap height, width, stroke contrast, terminals, rhythm; make the difference obvious enough to look intentional; test both in the same sentence, button, price and interface crop; adjust optical size, weight and scale before changing families. Avoid two almost-identical grotesks, a display serif used only to imitate "premium", and a mono face as atmospheric metadata.
  **Why** Pairing method.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Pairing method".

- **RULE [LSV-26]** [team research] SaaS product UI type scale: dashboard title 24-32 px / line height 1.15-1.25 / weight 550-650; section heading 18-22 / 1.25-1.35 / 550-650; body 14-16 / 1.45-1.6 / 400-450; label 13-14 / 1.3-1.45 / 450-600; caption 12-13 / 1.35-1.5 / 400-500.
  **Why** Starting ranges for operational UI.
  **Applies to** SaaS app UI. **Check** computed sizes within ranges. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Type-scale starting points".

- **RULE [LSV-27]** [team research] For a 1440 px marketing hero start headline exploration around 64-96 px, not an automatic 140-180 px; set final size from copy length, line shape, proof balance and screenshot scale; keep body copy around 18-22 px when it must survive a social downscale; aim for 2-4 headline lines only when the breaks make a deliberate silhouette; `text-wrap: balance` for short headings and `pretty` for supporting copy, manual breaks only for art direction.
  **Why** Starting ranges, not universal rules.
  **Applies to** landing page, hero concept stills (1440 canvas). **Check** H1 64-96 px at 1440; lede 18-22 px. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-28]** [team research] Production type: serve WOFF2; load only used styles/axes; a variable font pays off when several weights/widths/optical sizes replace multiple static files (one variable file can be larger than one static face); prefer CSS `font-weight`, `font-stretch`, `font-optical-sizing` over raw variation tags; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for changing or aligned numbers; preload only critical fonts; subset with verified language coverage; match fallback metrics; do not disable font synthesis until real bold/italic/fallback behaviour is tested; let users override spacing without clipping (WCAG 2.2 text-spacing testing is an adaptability test, not a mandate to author at those values).
  **Why** Payload, CLS and localisation safety.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** network font list; `font-variant-numeric` on numeric columns. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Production rules".

- **RULE [LSV-29]** [team research] A wordmark is not just text in a font: start with a licensed typeface whose structure fits; adjust kerning optically (diagonal and round pairs); modify only a few meaningful features; keep counters at favicon-adjacent sizes; create separate positive and reversed optical versions if needed; outline for distribution while keeping an editable source; do not distort, indiscriminately tighten or add ligatures to signal "custom"; check the licence (OFL Reserved Font Names, redistribution) before using an unmodified font as a distributable logo.
  **Why** Wordmark craft and legal safety.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** logo SVG legible at 16/24/32 px. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Wordmark-specific typography".

- **RULE [LSV-30]** [team research] Typography preflight: headline readable at 25% scale; company name has an intentional silhouette; upper/lower case semantic not fashionable; smaller roles keep a readable weight; numerals stop columns shifting; Lithuanian diacritics match the design quality; measure capped around 60-75 characters for sustained reading; spacing can be increased without loss; first paint understandable before the brand font arrives.
  **Why** Checklist.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** items in `check.mjs`; `visual`. **Source** `02-typography-and-font-selection.md` "Typography preflight".

### 2c. Product UI as proof

- **RULE [LSV-40]** [team research] A product visual must answer: what is the user looking at; what changed or can change; what action can the user take; how does it support the page claim. If it cannot, it is identity artwork, not proof.
  **Why** Four-question test.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills, landing page. **Check** write the four answers. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-41]** [team research] Build one vertical slice, not a platform: one primary object, one input/source, one transformation, one approval/control, one result, one failure/recovery state (e.g. contract review: vendor agreement, uploaded PDF, clauses mapped to playbook, counsel accepts/edits/rejects, redline + decision log, unsupported scan).
  **Why** Prevents the fake-dashboard habit.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** slice listed in `DIRECTION.md`. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "Build one vertical slice".

- **RULE [LSV-42]** [team research] Sample data: names belong to one organisation; dates follow a plausible sequence; totals add up; statuses agree across views; IDs and currencies one format; charts correspond to visible records; empty/loading/error/success states use the same domain language. Label the visual `Sample workflow` or `Demo data`; do not use a tiny legal-looking label as camouflage for a misleading claim.
  **Why** The 2026-08-29 mock claimed 47 alerts collapsed into one incident while listing 49 silenced (preflight Gate 1).
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** sum reconciliation; label present at readable size. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md`; `playbooks/visual-deliverable-preflight.md` Gate 1, Gate 6.

- **RULE [LSV-43]** [team research] Choose a product-stage composition from the mechanism: focus crop (near-real scale, one control/result relationship); state sequence (two or three states of one object); source-to-result bridge (input and output opposite, mechanism between); live deterministic demo (small real interaction with fixed data and text transcript); annotated proof (two or three annotations tied to exact UI evidence; avoid scattered floating callout cards).
  **Why** Mechanism-led staging.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "Product-stage composition".

- **RULE [LSV-44]** [team research] Use a marketing frame only to crop, mask or place the product, not to add an ornamental second window system; do not put every visual inside a rounded browser frame; device frames only when hardware or responsive behaviour is part of the story; shadows and borders follow one lighting and elevation model.
  **Why** Marketing chrome vs application chrome.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-45]** [team research] Define a minimal product system before the mockup: neutral and semantic colour roles; 4 or 8 px spacing base; type roles; control heights and hit targets; border/divider/elevation/radius rules; icon stroke and size rules; focus, hover, selected, disabled, loading, empty, error, success states; density behaviour. The marketing page may be expressive; the embedded product stays operational.
  **Why** Consistent UI inside the frame.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** tokens file. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "Product-system foundations".

- **RULE [LSV-46]** [team research] Prefer visible causality in a hero (source line connected to its result; before value and approved after value; an edit with version history; an alert with its evidence; a timeline where automation stops for human review; a concrete object reaching a completed state). Avoid charts without a decision, activity feeds of random names, giant "AI score" rings, progress bars for no real process, floating cards separated from their workflow, unlabeled typing/generation simulations, invented speed/accuracy/savings/revenue numbers.
  **Why** Proof vs decoration.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "What to reveal in a hero".

- **RULE [LSV-47]** [team research] Motion as explanation: control feedback around 160-220 ms; larger state changes roughly 420-700 ms only when the added time helps comprehension; under reduced motion render the settled state immediately and keep the sequence understandable.
  **Why** Do not slow the product to create a cinematic clip.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills with interaction. **Check** transition durations in ranges. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "Motion as explanation".

- **RULE [LSV-48]** [team research] Product-proof acceptance: workflow describable accurately in one sentence; every chart/metric/status/action has domain meaning; sample relationships consistent; static first frame still proves something; interaction has loading, cancel, retry, failure and completion where relevant; product text readable at feed scale; no unsupported customer, security, compliance, availability or performance claim.
  **Why** Acceptance list.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual` + reconciliation. **Source** `03-product-ui-as-proof.md` "Product-proof acceptance".

### 2d. Logo and wordmark

- **RULE [LSV-50]** [team research] Begin a logo with three semantic territories (nouns, verbs, structures, materials, tensions) derived from product facts, each with a named risk (e.g. version history: strata / lineage / revision marks); test which best supports name, audience and future product family.
  **Why** A logo begins with a territory, not an icon.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** three territories listed. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-51]** [team research] Exploration method: confirm name, pronunciation, meaning, domain context and likely legal classes; check obvious existing company/product/domain/app-store conflicts early; draw at least 20 black-and-white thumbnails across three territories; remove generic category symbols (sparkles, hexagons, orbit rings, infinity loops, chat bubbles, rounded letter tiles, arbitrary gradients) unless product logic transforms them; select three directions by distinctiveness, relevance, simplicity, system potential; construct in vector; apply optical corrections; test small, reversed, monochrome, blurred and beside competitors; pair with a custom-kerned wordmark.
  **Why** Prevents the AI-logo failure modes.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** >= 20 thumbnails archived (when a logo is in scope). **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "Exploration method".

- **RULE [LSV-52]** [team research] Score marks 1-5 on semantic relevance, immediate silhouette, distinctiveness in category, legibility at 16, 24 and 32 px, positive/reversed performance, wordmark relationship, motion potential, icon/favicon/app-tile performance, extensibility, confusion risk. Reject a mark that scores through explanation alone.
  **Why** Selection scorecard.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** scorecard. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-53]** [team research] Optical construction: round shapes may overshoot cap line or baseline; diagonals may need different apparent weights than verticals; positive and reversed artwork may need separate stripe/gap/counter adjustments; internal negative spaces must survive small rendering and dark backgrounds; a mathematically centred symbol may need optical offset; derive clear space from a stable feature (cap height, mark width, a module). IBM's identity is cited as proof that systematic marks use different positive and reversed details; do not copy geometry.
  **Why** Geometry is a scaffold; perception decides.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "Optical construction".

- **RULE [LSV-54]** [team research] Minimum concept identity deliverables: primary horizontal lockup; standalone symbol or documented decision not to use one; wordmark; positive, reversed, monochrome artwork; favicon/small-icon version; clear-space rule; minimum-size rule; colour roles; one example on the marketing header and one inside the product; SVG master and PNG exports; one-paragraph rationale and misuse list. A real launch also needs trademark/legal review, source ownership records, full export package, co-branding rules, partner usage, app-store assets, social avatars, motion rules, print colour specs.
  **Why** Definition of a finished identity.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** file list. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "Identity family".

- **RULE [LSV-55]** [team research] SVG logos: informative inline SVG gets `role="img"` and a short accessible name (visible text with `aria-labelledby` or a first-child `<title>`); decorative repeats `aria-hidden="true"`; avoid duplicate announcements when a linked logo already has a brand/home label; stable `viewBox`; simplified paths with editor metadata removed; keep an editable source and do not round-trip flattened SVG.
  **Why** Accessibility and maintainability.
  **Applies to** all. **Check** DOM audit of logo SVG. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "SVG and accessibility".

- **RULE [LSV-56]** [team research] Never describe a concept name or mark as legally available or trademark-cleared; a web search is not a clearance review. For real brands search exact and similar names, alternative spellings/pronunciations, design codes, related goods and services, app stores, domains, company databases, common-law use, reverse image search as a screen; consult a trademark professional before adoption (USPTO guidance cited).
  **Why** Originality and trademark gate.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills, real brands. **Check** concept note states "not cleared". **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "Originality and trademark gate".

- **RULE [LSV-57]** [team research] Avoid AI-logo failure modes: a generic geometric icon rationalised after drawing; a sparkle as AI shorthand; the first letter in a rounded square; four gradients before a monochrome test; perfect symmetry with weak optical weight; a logo that cannot be redrawn because it depends on texture; a custom wordmark made by deleting random pieces of letters; a mark too close to a well-known design defended as "inspired"; a concept presented as trademark-safe.
  **Why** Failure list.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `04-logo-and-wordmark-system.md` "AI-logo failure modes".

### 2e. Shaders, backgrounds, effects

- **RULE [LSV-60]** [team research] Invent an effect with: brand concept + visual primitive + transformation + input + constraint (e.g. reconciliation + braided paths + merge into one traceable line + scroll progress + never cross the copy column). Effect families and their generic failures: SDF (endless metaballs), domain warping (cloudy purple noise), flow fields (particle soup), Voronoi (tech-honeycomb), reaction-diffusion (decorative slime), advection (cursor goo everywhere), raymarching (chrome blob), refraction (glass-card distortion), dither/halftone (grain over everything), contour (random map lines), interference/moire (illegible vibration), image displacement (unrelated cursor ripple), particles (starfield "AI"), procedural material (texture hiding weak composition), grid/terrain (Tron grid).
  **Why** Effects need a semantic job.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** formula sentence recorded. **Source** `05-shader-and-background-atlas.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-61]** [team research] Choose one dominant effect lane: subtle depth field; system behaviour; object-centric 3D; cinematic world. Do not mix three or four heavy lanes in the first viewport; a SaaS home page usually needs subtle depth or system behaviour; pricing and high-intent pages need less.
  **Why** Visual noise and cost.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** lane named. **Source** `05-shader-and-background-atlas.md` "One dominant lane".

- **RULE [LSV-62]** [team research] Effect composition: design the static poster first; reserve a quiet contrast region for copy; lower motion amplitude near text and actions; give pointer response a bounded purpose (never make visitors chase the CTA); one dominant frequency scale (not fine grain + medium particles + large blobs + moving grids); keep colours inside the brand tonal hierarchy; share geometry with logo or product model; stop the effect at a meaningful boundary.
  **Why** Subordinate the effect to the page.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `05-shader-and-background-atlas.md` "Composition rules".

- **RULE [LSV-63]** [team research] Anti-generic effect test, answer all eight: what product fact generated it; what would change if the company sold something else; what is the primitive; what is transforming; what user input matters; what constraint keeps it subordinate; can it be recognised in one still frame; can it fall back to a static image without losing the message. If the first two answers are weak it is decoration and stays simple.
  **Why** Effect-as-decoration filter.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** eight answers in `DIRECTION.md`. **Source** `05-shader-and-background-atlas.md` "Anti-generic test".

- **RULE [LSV-64]** [team research] Effect libraries: Three.js for full scenes; React Three Fiber for declarative React composition; OGL for a lighter WebGL layer; native WebGL2 for small full-screen fragment effects; WebGPU/TSL only with compatibility and fallback planning; The Book of Shaders, LYGIA (dual-licence, do not assume unrestricted commercial use), Inigo Quilez articles, MDN WebGL best practices as learning sources. Do not select from demo beauty alone: check licence, bundle weight, maintenance, framework fit, cleanup, fallback, tunability.
  **Why** Library selection criteria.
  **Applies to** tooling. **Check** n/a. **Source** `05-shader-and-background-atlas.md` "Libraries and learning sources".

- **RULE [LSV-65]** [team research] Build effects progressively: semantic content and layout; static poster or CSS background; capability and motion-preference checks; lazy-loaded renderer; interaction and animation; adaptive quality; cleanup and context-loss handling. Message and action must work before the renderer initialises and after it fails. Three DOM layers: `hero__fallback` (visible immediately), `hero__canvas` (decorative, usually `aria-hidden="true"`, non-interactive, `pointer-events: none`), `hero__content` (semantic). Never put essential text in Canvas/WebGL.
  **Why** Progressive enhancement.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** DOM layers present; hero readable with JS disabled. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-66]** [team research] Quality tiers: static (reduced motion, unsupported contexts, data-saving, low-power, failure); low (lower resolution, no post-processing, fewer particles, reduced update rate, no pointer history); standard (devicePixelRatio capped around 1.5, one or two meshes/passes, modest particles, bounded pointer response); high (capable desktops only, when it materially benefits). Never make the high tier the only art-directed version. Start with `min(devicePixelRatio, 1.5)` for continuous full-bleed effects; allow 2 only after profiling; render a smaller back buffer and upscale for soft/noisy effects.
  **Why** Pixel budget.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** `renderer.setPixelRatio(min(dpr,1.5))` or equivalent. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md` "Quality tiers", "Resolution policy".

- **RULE [LSV-67]** [team research] Render-loop policy: use `setAnimationLoop` where applicable; pause when not visible; render on demand for static scenes; no per-frame allocation; clamp delta time after tab suspension; stop observers/listeners at teardown; dispose geometries, materials, textures, render targets and renderer on route change; handle `webglcontextlost`.
  **Why** Performance and leak safety.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** code audit. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md` "Render-loop policy".

- **RULE [LSV-68]** [team research] Fragment-background recipe: one screen-covering triangle; pass resolution, time, normalised pointer uniforms; correct aspect ratio; few noise octaves; consistent colour space; authored contrast mask behind the content column; quantise/dither/grain only if it belongs to the identity; capture a deterministic poster; use seeded randomness. Bind the visual to meaningful state (workflow stage, selected source/result, scroll progress, pointer proximity to a relevant object, completion/approval/conflict) mapped to one or two parameters.
  **Why** Reproducible approved first frame.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** poster capture exists. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-69]** [team research] Fallback is part of the design: capture at the approved composition (not a random frame); export AVIF/WebP plus a compatible option if needed; keep the same quiet copy region and contrast; separate desktop/mobile crops if composition changes; keep the poster loaded while the first shader frame compiles then crossfade only after a successful render; under reduced motion keep the still or a near-static low-amplitude state.
  **Why** Graceful degradation.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** poster asset. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md` "Fallback capture".

- **RULE [LSV-70]** [team research] Performance and accessibility gates for effects: no long task delaying navigation/CTA; no layout shift when the canvas initialises; no uncontrolled VRAM growth; no repeated shader compilation after load; no WebGL errors; avoid blocking calls (`getParameter`, readbacks); respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; preserve text contrast across every animated frame; avoid rapid flashing/high-frequency interference; provide a DOM/transcript equivalent for meaningful sequences; keep keyboard/pointer actions independent of canvas; never encode state only through hue or motion; zoom and text-spacing changes must not collide with a fixed canvas composition.
  **Why** Gates.
  **Applies to** landing page. **Check** profiling on a representative phone and laptop. **Source** `06-shader-implementation-recipes.md`.

### 2f. Routing, preflight, stop conditions

- **RULE [LSV-80]** [team research] Route product situations to type direction, proof, logo territory, behaviour and bans (excerpt): developer infrastructure -> high-legibility grotesk, mono only for real code; proof request/route/trace/result; avoid black + neon grid + code wallpaper. Security/privacy -> calm sturdy sans; protected object/evidence/action/recovery; avoid shield/lock cliche and threatening red. Finance/operations -> stable proportions, excellent numerals; source values/reconciliation/approval/audit trail; avoid crypto gradients, fake growth. Creative collaboration -> expressive display + usable UI face; avoid rainbow blob, floating avatars. Climate/science -> transparent info-first type; avoid simulated data as evidence. HR/team workflow -> human not childish; avoid pastel people illustrations. AI writing/research -> strong reading face; sources, intermediate state, edit, citation, output; avoid purple orb, sparkle, fake typing. Healthcare -> quiet legible accessible type, conservative motion; avoid glossy wellness. Consumer productivity -> compact warm fast type; avoid gradient mesh + oversized rounded cards. Premium B2B service -> distinctive controlled display + sober UI; avoid generic serif + beige + thin rules.
  **Why** Decision matrix (not a style generator; every row needs product-specific facts).
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** `visual`. **Source** `07-brand-to-visual-decision-matrix.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-81]** [team research] Five-question router: is the effect proof, explanation, interaction or identity; which product verb should it embody; which visual primitive carries that verb; what familiar structure keeps the page understandable; which one deviation makes the system difficult to transplant. Generate three systems that change type structure, composition, logo territory and signature behaviour together, not three colour variants.
  **Why** Routing premise to system.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** three systems recorded. **Source** `07-brand-to-visual-decision-matrix.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-82]** [team research] Fill the product-derived vocabulary worksheet before exploring: product nouns; verbs; user emotions before/after; physical analogies; structures; materials; time behaviour; inputs; outputs; human-control point; risk or objection; category cliches to ban; familiar page frame; authored deviation.
  **Why** Product-derived visual vocabulary.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** worksheet present. **Source** `07-brand-to-visual-decision-matrix.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-83]** [team research] SaaS hero preflight groups: product truth (all facts written; coherent sample data and a real limitation; labelled sample workflow; no invented logos/testimonials/certifications/metrics); typography (licence, coverage, axes, WOFF2; Lithuanian glyph test; 25% readability; role-based styles; no damaging layout shift); product visual (explains its own object, state change, action; believable density; coherent data; states exist); logo (3 territories + 20 thumbnails; one colour/reversed/16-32 px; custom kerning; preliminary conflict checks recorded; never "legally cleared"); effect (derived from a noun/verb; one lane; approved poster first; quiet copy region stable; reduced-motion/mobile/low-power/failure fallbacks; pixel ratio/iterations/pause/cleanup explicit); composition (1440 x 1024 directly; survives cropping; focal order survives squint/grayscale; nav and CTA geometry same brand system; one memorable deviation).
  **Why** Preflight.
  **Applies to** hero concept stills. **Check** checklist. **Source** `08-preflight-and-skill-routing.md`.

- **RULE [LSV-84]** [team research] Do not proceed to polish when: the product cannot be explained as a workflow; the chosen font lacks glyphs or web rights; the logo has not survived monochrome and small-size tests; the effect has no product-derived rationale; the screenshot uses contradictory or fake data; the static first frame is weaker than the animation; the CTA and navigation are generic components pasted over an unrelated visual.
  **Why** Stop conditions.
  **Applies to** process. **Check** n/a. **Source** `08-preflight-and-skill-routing.md` "Stop conditions".

- **RULE [LSV-85]** [team research] Use one primary skill plus no more than two supporting skills (routing table: product-led hero -> `product-proof-saas` + `better-typography` + `better-layout`; font system -> `better-typography` + `better-colors` + `better-accessibility`; generic-looking audit -> `audit-ai-design-slop` + `no-ai-design-slop` + `audit-reference-originality`; subtle WebGL hero -> `webgl-landing-steering` + `webgl-components` + `optimize-web-animations`; custom Three.js -> `threejs` + `webgl-3d-object`; final interaction critique -> `better-interface` + `web-quality-audit` + `review-animations`). Start from the concept, not the skill name; a skill that says "add particles" is a technique source, not authorisation.
  **Why** Skill routing principle.
  **Applies to** process. **Check** n/a. **Source** `08-preflight-and-skill-routing.md`.

## 5. Exceptions & judgement calls

Resolution: allow a common family only with a written product reason and never across more than two batch concepts.
- The 1440 x 1024 frame (LSV-08) was superseded on 2026-08-30 by 1920 x 1080 / 3840 x 2160 for X stills.
- This pack's "fictional concept" standard (labelled sample, no invented proof) is the same truth boundary the constraints require (see `brand-constraints.md`).
