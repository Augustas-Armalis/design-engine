# Halyard mark: concepts (drawing-method step 1; LD-01, LD-23)

A halyard is the line that hoists a sail. The product: an on-call agent that finds the cause, asks, and brings a system back up.

| # | source | concept (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | name | A sail mid-hoist: a straight luff and a full, curved leech, the blade sitting high on an implied mast. |
| 2 | name + verb | Two sails, one raised above the other: a jib and a main, offset by a step, the step reading as "going up". |
| 3 | letter | An H whose crossbar is a hoisted bar: the bar sits high and tilted, pulled up the right stem. |
| 4 | letter + name | An H made of a mast (left stem, straight) and a sail (right stem, a curved blade): the letter and the boat in one shape. |
| 5 | verb | The halyard itself: one tapered line that runs up and turns over a sheave, a hook with weight at the bottom and a fine free end. |
| 6 | figure-ground | Two blades facing each other whose negative space between them is an H. |
| 7 | product | The incident curve brought down: a mass with a single bite taken out of its top-right shoulder, the bite the shape of a falling line. |
| 8 | product + verb | A pennant raised on a line: a small flag at the top of a stem, the flag cut by the scan line. |
| 9 | letter | A pixel H from the product's cells, with the crossbar cell lifted one step (the current placeholder, refined). |

Struck before sketching: shield (security cliché), bell or pager (literal), pulse/heartbeat line (observability cliché), anything inside a circle or rounded square (LD-20 signage test).
